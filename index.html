Part 2 · Financial ML and algorithmic trading

Theory and maths from scratch, then end-to-end projects on real market data, each one harder than the last. Every chapter has a guide, a notebook that reproduces every number and figure, the dataset, and an interactive playground.

  • 1Theory

    The Statistics of Financial Returns

    Returns and stationarity, fat tails, volatility clustering, autocorrelation tests, maximum likelihood, the bootstrap and block bootstrap, and why one backtest proves almost nothing (multiple testing, the deflated Sharpe ratio).

    Open →
  • 2Project

    A Backtester from Scratch: Trend Following

    Build an honest vectorised backtester, test time-series momentum on real ETFs, add costs and volatility targeting, walk forward, and measure how much of the result is luck.

    Open →
  • 3Project

    Forecasting Volatility

    Realised volatility, EWMA, GARCH(1,1) fitted by maximum likelihood from scratch, HAR and gradient boosting, judged with the right loss, then used to size positions.

    Open →
  • 4Project

    Portfolio Construction out of Sample

    Mean–variance and why it fails on real estimates, shrinkage, minimum variance, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity, compared in a walk-forward test.

    Open →
  • 5Theory + project

    Labels (fixed horizon, triple barrier), meta-labelling, overlapping samples, purged and embargoed cross-validation, feature importance and the probability of backtest overfitting.

    Cross-Sectional Factors and ML Ranking

    Momentum, reversal and low-risk signals across a stock universe, information coefficients, Fama–MacBeth regressions, long–short portfolios, and ML rankers judged after costs.

Each chapter folder holds index.html (the guide), a notebook, images/, datasets/ and animations/. The guides and notebooks are generated from one source, chapter.md, by tools/build.py.

Educational material, not investment advice. Backtests are not predictions; markets change.
